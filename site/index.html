Jane: a learning buddy that plans a new adventure every night

Jane is a free, friendly learning app for kids in grades 3 to 6 (about ages 8 to 12). Your kid plays a short quest in a web browser. Overnight, your own AI agent looks at how it went and writes the next one.

No account to sign up for, no ads, and no AI chatting with your kid. Jane runs in your own setup, and your kid's progress stays there.

Got Grok Bot?

Make a bot named Jane, point it at this repo, and it'll set up and run an adaptive learning program for your grade-schooler. Jane is built on the Marble skill map, and the bot writes fresh lessons every night based on how your kid did the day before.

Make a Jane bot See the code on GitHub

Not using Grok Bot? Any AI agent that can run small programs on a schedule can do it, or you can run Jane yourself. See Get started.

What your kid gets

Short quests

About 8 minutes a day: a story, a few stops along the way, and a boss battle at the end, with a medal to win.

Teaching first

New ideas start with a quick lesson and an example, then practice with hints, and only then the real questions.

Help when stuck

An "I'm stuck" button gives a hint, then a worked example, then a free skip. Nobody loses a heart for asking.

Things come back at the right time

Skills your kid learned come back a day, a few days, a week and a month later, so they stick.

Medals that mean something

Medals are earned by getting questions right on the first try, never just by clicking through.

Lessons that adapt

Each night the next quest goes deeper where your kid struggled, stretches where they shine and visits something new.

The big idea in one picture

The app your kid uses has no AI inside it. The "brain" that adapts the lessons is your own AI agent, and it works from the outside, at night, while everyone sleeps. We call this Agent-Orchestrated Software. Here's how it works.

Built on a map of what kids learn

Jane uses the open Marble Skill Taxonomy: 1,590 small topics across 8 subjects (math, science, English, history, personal and social development, life skills, computing, and learning to learn), connected by 3,221 "learn this first" links. That map tells Jane where your kid is, what comes next and what to review.

Why "Jane"?

The friendly tutor is named after Jane, the AI companion and teacher in Orson Scott Card's Speaker for the Dead. It's just a nod to the books; the project has no connection to the author or his publishers.
